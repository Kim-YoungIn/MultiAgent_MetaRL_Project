# Choosing an experiment on the use of new interaction data

The original proposal combines changes in rules, opponent policies and opponent learning. A comparison of two returns identifies a policy choice for that deployment population. It does not identify which of those changes provided the benefit. We chose to estimate two quantities separately: the reward of a decision after a shared interaction history at a shared world state, and the return from independent live play where the two sides affect one another. For both, the independent replicate includes the draw of a training catalog. This choice followed a separate design critique before evaluating the new panels.

| Candidate | What can adapt | Discriminating comparison and scientific cost |
|---|---|---|
| Recurrent policy in self-play | Hidden activations can implement a learned filter, update rule, probe or shaping strategy. Few assumptions about the latent mechanism. | Reset or interchange histories while fixing opponent and game states. Both policies' training populations evolve together in self-play. Many outer-loop runs are needed to separate policy optimization variance from transfer. |
| Gradient-based meta-learning | An initialization or optimizer makes a few updates useful on a new task. Can adapt outside a finite type library. | Same architecture, ordinary multitask pretraining and identical optimizer calls on the same transitions. Credit assignment and changing opponent policies affect each gradient estimate. |
| Latent inference with learned likelihood | Can infer dynamics outside a small hand-coded library if the encoder learned a sufficient representation. Can train a belief-conditioned controller for exploration. | Test prediction calibration and posterior-conditioned actions on shared histories. Latent coordinates alone need not have a strategic interpretation. Training the representation and controller together introduces two possible sources of gain. |
| Population prior with exact within-game filtering | Learns how frequently strategic features go together. Eight candidate opponent states suffice in the two-arena experiment. There is an exact prediction for when unused-arena transfer should disappear or reverse. | Give the non-meta filter identical likelihoods and state updates. Compare at zero experience, on the same probe history, and under a reversed type distribution. Strong assumptions about opponent mechanisms limit external validity. |
| Hierarchical reward regression | Fits task-grouped prior coefficients. Recursive regression adapts them from observed payoff samples even when the rule likelihood is unavailable. | Compare task grouping with pooled pretraining plus identical updates, and both with scratch and frozen predictors. Very cheap to replicate across catalogs. Its immediate-reward objective can miss costly state preparation. |
| Meta-learned regret minimization | Reuses structure in games to accelerate online updates or equilibrium computation. | An appropriate endpoint is regret or time to a solution under specified feedback. Equilibrium convergence need not imply a rapid improvement in payoff against a specific adapting opponent. |
| Teacher–student exploration | Learns which behaviors provide useful data to a separate updating policy. | Holding learner and opponent state separately fixed is valuable. Its architecture is most informative when the game actually rewards a costly information-gathering choice. Enumerate such opportunities in a small game first. |

These alternatives have different comparison targets. Recurrent policies and learned update rules remain plausible for longer-horizon experiments in this generator. The two present architectures expose the part learned across tasks, the information received online and a competitive alternative that can be run on every identical held-out state. The exact diagnostic then measures how much the one-step controller might miss.

## Primary literature informing the comparison

- RL² stores the fast learning process in recurrent activations. Mali extends self-play meta-RL to distributions of normal-form games, with convergence and varying action spaces among the outcomes. For our question, a learning counterpart motivates separating the current-state action query from live interaction. [Duan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02779) · [Mali](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44427-026-00021-y)
- MAML trains initial parameters for downstream gradient updates. Al-Shedivat et al. study adaptation to changing competitors including in RoboSumo. Both motivate an ordinary pretrained comparator with equal online optimization. [Finn et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/finn17a.html) · [Al-Shedivat et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03641)
- PEARL separates probabilistic task inference from control. VariBAD conditions control on task uncertainty, which can justify information-seeking actions. The action query in our main study deliberately measures a one-step decision. Our two-turn diagnostic gives information a future use. [Rakelly et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/rakelly19a.html) · [Zintgraf et al.](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v22/21-0657.html)
- Meta-Thompson Sampling learns a prior across bandit instances. The present filter similarly tests prior estimation, but must advance candidate opponent states using past focal actions. Bandit regret results do not transfer automatically. [Kveton et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/kveton21a.html)
- Meta-learning in games relates game similarity to equilibrium computation. Work on targeting diversity in open-ended simulators motivates defining a meaningful diversity intervention. We vary strategic sign dependence at fixed marginal signs and separately vary learner-rate coverage and game count. [Harris et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14110) · [Costales and Nikolaidis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04466)
- Model-free opponent shaping uses an outer objective that can reward influencing a learner. We instead let learning opponents update naturally during both shared probes and deployment, and measure their behavior alongside focal return. [Lu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/lu22d.html)
