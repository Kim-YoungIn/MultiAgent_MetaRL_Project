## Task and mechanism

Each task is a simultaneous repeated game with two publicly signaled arenas c and two actions per player. A fresh game supplies two public payoff strengths g drawn independently uniformly on [1.5,3.5] and signed focal action-1 bonuses d with independent fair signs and magnitudes uniform on [.05,.20]. The focal payoff is 1[a=b]+d_c a. The opponent payoff is s_c g_c 1[a=b]. Positive s favors coordination and negative s favors anti-coordination. Its boundedly rational policy responds to its estimate m of the focal action, initialized at .5 on the signed action scale. Before seeing the current focal move, its probability of action 1 is sigmoid(s_c g_c m). After both act it updates m ← (1−λ)m + λ(2a−1), an exponentially weighted form of fictitious play. λ is .12 or .65. Thus changing either player's action can change the other's payoff, and focal action histories change the opponent's future play. No controller optimizes a shaping objective.

The hidden sign in the first arena is fair. With probability q the signs agree. The learning rate is independently fast with probability r. Fit only q and r from joint trajectories grouped by training game. Exact filtering over eight sign-pair/rate candidates uses observed public parameters, arena, and past joint actions. No private type labels or opponent probabilities enter training. Public payoff scales and bonuses define continuously new game instances, while the four strategic sign patterns recur. Transfer means using encounters in one arena to respond in a previously unvisited arena of the same new task. It does not require extrapolating an arbitrary new payoff language.

For known rate and source sign posterior, the predicted deviation from a fair action in the other arena is multiplied by 2q−1, with a separate sigmoid term for that arena's strength. This follows by averaging over concordant and discordant signs and using sigmoid(x)+sigmoid(−x)=1. At q=.5 no source history informs the other sign. At zero experience all priors give the same opponent prediction of .5. Signed focal action bonuses give the context-free policy a genuine decision and charge a cost for incorrectly overriding it. Learning a higher-order prior can therefore change context use even with exactly matched initial decisions. The likelihood and update procedure are supplied inductive biases.

## Training interventions

Fully cross 32 or 128 distinct games, concordance q=1,.75,.5 and fast fraction r=0,.5. Each arm gets 128 independent resets of 16 interactions, allocated evenly to games. Stratify hidden types to those exact proportions including fair signs, with common public games and exogenous random draws across q/r at a given count. Fixed budget count comparisons trade unique games against repeats per game. Support expands between q=1 and .75, whereas .75 and .5 have the same support and different weights. Rate diversity changes exposure to fast learners. Count, conditional pattern mix and opponent-rate mix are distinct interventions. Training uses independent fair arenas and a persistent random focal policy (four-turn blocks with a fair preferred bit followed independently with probability .85). No method gets additional encounters. All training games have labels identifying repeated resets of that game. Trajectories within a game share a type. Fit the two mixture parameters by 80 EM iterations from (.5,.5), adding one pseudo-count to each side of both Bernoulli proportions (Beta(2,2) MAP). Store inputs, likelihoods and parameters.

## Controls and measurements

All deployable controllers observe the same public fields. Each myopically chooses its expected-reward maximizing action, using action 0 at exact ties. The independent filter has q=r=.5 and learns within each arena. The frozen learned filter retains its symmetric prior weights. A same-data pooled logistic predictor estimates action probability using arena, public strength and own-action traces, without opponent-specific outcome updates. A conventional Laplace-smoothed per-arena opponent frequency learner uses only local counts. Population oracles use q=.75,.5,.25 with r=.5 and update normally. A privileged type oracle knows the actual opponent mechanism, signs and rate. It bounds one-step value at a shared state, with no optimality claim about long-horizon play.

Each independent block has a fresh training catalog and test set. For each of the eight sign/rate strata draw eight fresh games (64 total), using separate random streams for training and evaluation. Reweight identical stratified test tasks to deployment concordances .75,.5,.25, always with balanced rates. For common probes, draw a fair source arena and 16 fixed focal moves Bernoulli(.8), updating the opponent after each simultaneous round. At k=0,1,2,4,8,16 evaluate a hypothetical one-step decision in each arena from that same state, without feeding the query back. Record exact expected focal reward and expected Brier score (lower is better), oracle and frozen comparisons. The secondary deployment is a fresh reset, 32 steps with independent fair arenas and paired opponent uniforms, tracking joint actions, rewards, opponent probabilities and belief magnitudes. An additional paired misspecification test uses separate opponent beliefs per arena, each updated only when visited. Filters still assume one global belief. All resets use fresh state.
