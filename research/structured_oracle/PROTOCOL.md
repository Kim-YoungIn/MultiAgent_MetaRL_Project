# Two-turn information and preparation in the structured extension

This diagnostic was designed after the two adaptation studies and before drawing this separate game panel. Its purpose is to measure decision opportunities supplied by the executable generator. Its controller has privileged knowledge of two possible rule specifications and their equal prior. These are properties of a benchmark under stated information assumptions.

Each pair contains independent generated rules of the same motif. Both begin at their prescribed initial state with identical seeded action history. Play the real transition rules. At each step a fresh epsilon-greedy tabular Q opponent responds simultaneously, using its previous own and focal actions as its state, starting from zero and updating with alpha=.3, gamma=.8 and epsilon=.25. Its first action is uniform. After one round the affected entry is .3 times its reward, the other entries are zero, and its second action distribution is evaluated in its actual next state. The focal policy knows the hypotheses and mechanism, observes the joint move and public state and, in the full condition, its own first reward. The private trust value and opponent reward are hidden. Expectations average opponent actions, rule hypotheses and stochastic state-transition gates; exact reward numbers are observed without noise. Hidden rule identity can be easy to distinguish through payoff precision.

For each possible first move compute expected immediate payoff and the maximum expected second payoff conditional on each observable outcome, summing probability-weighted action values inside observation groups before maximizing. Compare (1) optimal two-turn value with versus without observing the first focal reward, allowing both strategies to observe joint actions and public state, and (2) full-information-protocol value versus the best two-turn policy whose first action must maximize immediate expected reward. The second contrast includes physical state preparation, opponent responses and information acquisition. It cannot isolate costly exploration. Also report the analogous first-action restriction in the reward-masked condition. Numerical tolerance for positive gains and immediate ties is 1e-10.

Use fresh seed 61739291, 24 independent pairs per motif (resource, sequence, reciprocity, mixed). Compare three actions with depth 1 and depth 3 on paired generator and history seeds, then six actions with depth 5 using those seeds. Action count and depth change together in the last setting. Reward/transition grammar and learner rule are shared. Report every pair, positive counts and mean gaps by setting and motif; no inference of a population prevalence or ranking against previously opened D-031 panels. Deterministic unit fixtures additionally exercise information grouping, opponent updating and reward evaluation against the original simulator. No full history tensor or full game-state enumeration is needed.
